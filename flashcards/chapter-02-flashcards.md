## Chapter 2: AI Asset, Data Lifecycle, and Policy Management — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | NIST AI Risk Management Framework — how many core functions, and name them? | 4 functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Exam tip: 'Govern' underpins the other three and is the only function applied continuously across the entire lifecycle. |
| 2 | When was ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management system standard) published? | December 2023. Exam tip: it's the first certifiable AI management system standard, structured like ISO 27001 with a management system clause + Annex A controls. |
| 3 | EU AI Act — how many risk tiers, and name them? | 4 tiers: Unacceptable (banned), High-risk (strict obligations), Limited risk (transparency duties), Minimal risk (no specific obligations). Exam tip: classification is scenario-driven — know which use cases fall in each tier. |
| 4 | EU AI Act key compliance dates? | Entered into force Aug 1, 2024. Prohibited practices applicable Feb 2, 2025. GPAI (general-purpose AI) obligations Aug 2, 2025. Most high-risk system obligations Aug 2, 2026 (some Annex I high-risk systems extended to Aug 2027). |
| 5 | Model Card vs. Datasheet for Datasets — what's the difference? | Model Card = documents a trained model (performance, limitations, intended use). Datasheet for Datasets = documents a dataset (collection method, composition, known bias). Exam tip: exam scenarios test which artifact matches the audit request. |
| 6 | Order the AI governance policy hierarchy from highest to lowest. | Strategy → Policy → Standard → Procedure → Guideline. Strategy = direction/why; Policy = mandatory rule; Standard = mandatory specifics; Procedure = step-by-step how; Guideline = optional recommendation. |
| 7 | What is 'Shadow AI'? | Unsanctioned use of AI tools/models outside the organization's approved inventory and governance controls (e.g., employees using public GenAI tools with sensitive data). Exam tip: remediation sequence is discover → inventory/classify risk → apply controls, not immediate punitive action. |
| 8 | What four asset types must an AI asset inventory scope include? | Models, datasets (training/eval), pipelines/orchestration code, and prompt templates. Exam tip: exam traps narrow the scope to 'models only' — always pick the answer that includes all four. |
| 9 | Name three common triggers for AI model/data decommissioning or retraining review. | 1) Regulatory/legal change creating a compliance gap, 2) significant model performance/data drift, 3) end-of-support for underlying data/vendor dependency or contract expiration. Exam tip: pick the trigger tied to compliance or risk, not cosmetic/operational changes. |
| 10 | Data Provenance vs. Data Lineage — how do they differ? | Provenance = origin and ownership history of the data (where it came from, who collected it). Lineage = the full transformation path data took through pipelines to reach its current state. Exam tip: 'lineage' answers usually involve pipeline/transformation steps; 'provenance' answers involve source/origin. |
| 11 | Why classify training data and inference/prompt data separately? | Training data risk relates to aggregated historical exposure and reproducibility; inference/prompt data risk relates to real-time PII/sensitive input exposure at point of use. Exam tip: scenario questions test recognizing prompt data as its own classification category, not lumped with training data. |
| 12 | Policy vs. Standard — one-line distinction? | Policy = mandatory rule ('what must happen'). Standard = mandatory measurable requirement supporting the policy ('exact criteria to meet it'). Exam tip: if the question gives a specific numeric/field requirement, the answer is 'standard', not 'policy'. |

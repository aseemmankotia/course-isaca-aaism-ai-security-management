## Chapter 10: Full AAISM Exam Simulation and Test-Taking Strategy — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | AAISM exam length and question count? | 180 minutes, 90 questions. Exam tip: average pace = 2 minutes/question. |
| 2 | Passing score threshold on the AAISM scale? | 450 out of 800. Exam tip: this is a scaled, domain-weighted score — not a raw percentage of correct answers. |
| 3 | Pacing checkpoint target at question 30 of 90? | Should be reached by ~60 minutes elapsed. Exam tip: if behind here, speed up on recall questions first, not scenario questions. |
| 4 | Pacing checkpoint target at question 60 of 90? | Should be reached by ~120 minutes elapsed. Exam tip: this leaves 60 minutes for the final 30 questions plus flagged reviews. |
| 5 | Scenario-style vs recall-style question — key difference? | Scenario = multi-constraint situation, pick the option satisfying ALL constraints. Recall = single fact/definition/limit. Exam tip: budget <1 min for recall, 2-4 min for scenario. |
| 6 | Flag-and-review rule under time pressure? | Always select a best-guess answer before flagging — never leave it blank. Exam tip: a flagged blank guarantees zero credit; a flagged guess preserves a chance. |
| 7 | Elimination strategy after removing 2 of 4 obviously wrong options? | Re-read the scenario's stated constraints (cost, compliance, scale, latency) to find the specific requirement that differentiates the two remaining options — don't guess yet. |
| 8 | Is 'longer/more technical-sounding option = correct' a valid heuristic? | No. Distractors are frequently padded with technical detail specifically to bait this assumption. Exam tip: judge options against stated constraints, not verbosity. |
| 9 | Cross-domain integration question — definition? | A question requiring knowledge from 2+ exam domains applied simultaneously to reach one best answer. Exam tip: often appears as a long scenario combining architecture with security or cost with compliance. |
| 10 | Final-week review checklist priority order? | 1) Timed full practice exam. 2) Review only missed/flagged practice questions. 3) Re-review heavily-tested domain summaries. 4) Skip cover-to-cover re-reading. Exam tip: passive re-reading yields the lowest score gain in the final week. |
| 11 | Time budget rule after finishing all 90 questions with flagged items remaining? | Spend all remaining time only on flagged questions, lowest-confidence first. Exam tip: never leave exam time unused — flagged-question review yields disproportionate score gains. |
| 12 | Scaled score 410/800 vs passing 450/800 — what does the gap mean? | It does NOT mean 40 more raw correct answers out of 800. Scores are normalized per domain difficulty, so identical raw performance can map to different scaled scores. Exam tip: use any domain-level breakdown to target weak areas. |

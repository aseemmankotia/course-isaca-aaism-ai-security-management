## Chapter 1: AI Governance Foundations: Frameworks as Program Inputs — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | NIST AI RMF: name the four core functions in order. | Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Exam tip: Govern is cross-cutting/continuous, not a sequential first step — it applies throughout the other three. |
| 2 | ISO/IEC 42001 vs NIST AI RMF — key structural difference? | ISO/IEC 42001 is a certifiable AI management system (AIMS) standard built on the Annex SL high-level structure (like ISO 27001). NIST AI RMF is voluntary and non-certifiable. Exam tip: 'certification' or 'audit' in a question points to ISO 42001. |
| 3 | MITRE ATLAS — what kind of framework is it? | An ATT&CK-style knowledge base of adversarial tactics and techniques against AI/ML systems. It is a threat-input framework, NOT a governance program framework — it feeds the Map/Measure functions of a program like NIST AI RMF. |
| 4 | OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications — what does it cover? | A ranked list of top LLM application security risks (e.g., prompt injection, insecure output handling, excessive agency, training data poisoning). Practical AppSec checklist for LLM apps, not an enterprise governance framework. |
| 5 | EU AI Act — name the four risk tiers. | Unacceptable (prohibited), High-risk (conformity assessment + full obligations), Limited risk (transparency obligations, e.g., chatbots/deepfakes), Minimal risk (no mandatory obligations). Exam tip: employment/recruitment AI = High-risk under Annex III regardless of how 'simple' the tool seems. |
| 6 | Which NIST AI RMF function covers establishing risk tolerance, culture, policy, and accountability structures? | Govern. Common mistake: confusing Govern (policy/accountability, cross-cutting) with Manage (resource allocation to treat prioritized risks). |
| 7 | Which NIST AI RMF function covers establishing context and categorizing an AI system's intended use and stakeholders? | Map. Exam tip: Map must occur before Measure — you can't meaningfully measure risk without first mapping context. |
| 8 | ISO/IEC 42001 structural basis? | Uses the Annex SL high-level structure shared with ISO 27001/9001, enabling integration with existing ISO management systems, plus AI-specific Annex A controls covering the AI system lifecycle, data, and impact assessment. |
| 9 | Framework selection: mandate vs. voluntary — how do NIST AI RMF/ISO 42001 relate to regulations like the EU AI Act? | Neither NIST AI RMF nor ISO 42001 is itself a legal mandate — both are voluntary structures used to operationalize compliance. A regulation like the EU AI Act defines WHAT must be done (legal obligations); the framework defines HOW to structure the program to meet it. |
| 10 | EU AI Act — is a generic customer-service chatbot automatically high-risk? | No. A basic chatbot is typically Limited risk, requiring only a transparency disclosure that users are interacting with AI. High-risk status depends on the specific Annex III use case (employment, credit scoring, biometrics, law enforcement, education access), not the 'chatbot' label alone. |
| 11 | Stakeholder mapping in AI governance — key groups to identify? | Internal: ML/data science teams, legal/compliance, security, business owners, executive sponsors. External: regulators, auditors, affected end users, third-party model/vendor providers. Exam tip: EU AI Act assigns distinct legal roles — provider, deployer, importer, distributor — each with different obligations. |
| 12 | Why is MITRE ATLAS a common exam trap when asked 'select a program framework'? | ATLAS is frequently listed as a distractor because it sounds authoritative and AI-security-related, but it is a threat/adversarial technique catalog, not a management or governance program framework — it cannot be the answer to 'which framework structures your governance program.' |

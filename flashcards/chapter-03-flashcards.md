## Chapter 3: AI Security Program Management, Training, BCP, and Incident Response — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | What are the four core functions of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)? | GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE. GOVERN is cross-cutting across the other three. Exam tip: GOVERN maps directly to AI security oversight/accountability structures. |
| 2 | How many phases does NIST SP 800-61 define in the incident response lifecycle? | 4: Preparation, Detection & Analysis, Containment/Eradication/Recovery, Post-Incident Activity. Exam tip: AI incident playbooks ADD steps within these phases (e.g., model forensics in Detection & Analysis) rather than replacing the lifecycle. |
| 3 | Three Lines Model — who owns AI risk at the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd line? | 1st line: data scientists/ML engineers (operational owners). 2nd line: AI governance/risk & compliance function (oversight). 3rd line: internal audit (independent assurance). Exam tip: assigning AI risk only to the 1st line with no 2nd-line oversight is a common exam trap. |
| 4 | ISO/IEC 42001 vs ISO/IEC 27001 — key difference? | ISO 42001 (published 2023) is the AI management system standard, addressing AI-lifecycle risks (data, model, bias, oversight). ISO 27001 addresses information security management generally. Exam tip: an org can hold both certifications; 42001 does not replace 27001. |
| 5 | AI Security Officer role vs CISO — exam-tested distinction? | AI Security Officer owns AI-lifecycle-specific risks (training data integrity, model robustness, drift, AI-specific compliance); CISO owns enterprise-wide security. Roles coordinate, not compete. Exam tip: they are complementary, not replacements. |
| 6 | KRI vs KPI — core distinction tested on the exam? | KRI = forward-looking measure of risk EXPOSURE (e.g., % of models with unresolved adversarial findings). KPI = measure of PROGRAM/PROCESS PERFORMANCE (e.g., % staff trained). Exam tip: training completion % is a KPI, NOT a KRI — commonly confused. |
| 7 | What is MITRE ATLAS and what does it map? | Adversarial Threat Landscape for Artificial-Intelligence Systems — catalogs real-world AI-specific attack tactics/techniques (e.g., data poisoning, model extraction, prompt injection), analogous to MITRE ATT&CK. Exam tip: use ATLAS to build AI-specific IR playbook scenarios. |
| 8 | Which AI-specific incident types must be added to a traditional IR playbook? | Data/training poisoning, model extraction/theft, adversarial evasion (input manipulation), prompt injection, model inversion (privacy leakage). Exam tip: traditional malware/phishing playbooks do not cover these — dedicated detection/forensics steps are required. |
| 9 | What triggers a BCP fallback for an AI-dependent business process? | Loss of model/API availability, significant model drift causing unacceptable output degradation, or detected data/model integrity compromise. Exam tip: BCP must define a manual or secondary-model fallback independent of vendor SLA terms. |
| 10 | How often should AI incident response tabletop exercises be conducted, per governance best practice? | At least annually, with additional exercises after major AI deployments or architecture changes. Exam tip: exercises must include AI-specific scenarios (e.g., model poisoning), not just generic breach scenarios. |
| 11 | What is the typical board reporting cadence for AI security KRIs/KPIs? | Quarterly, supplemented by ad hoc/event-driven reporting for significant risk events. Exam tip: annual-only reporting is considered insufficient oversight for a fast-evolving risk area like AI. |
| 12 | Why is role-based AI security training required instead of a single generic module? | Different roles face different AI risks: executives need risk/liability context, data scientists need secure model development practices, general staff need prompt-injection/shadow-AI awareness. Exam tip: 'one training for all' is the WRONG answer in exam scenarios. |

## Chapter 8: Data Management, Privacy, Ethics, and Human-Oversight Controls — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | Four-fifths (80%) rule — what does it flag and where does it originate? | Adverse impact indicated when a protected group's selection rate is less than 80% of the highest group's rate. Source: EEOC Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures. Exam tip: used to test AI hiring/screening tools for disparate impact. |
| 2 | NIST AI RMF 1.0 — name the four core functions | Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Govern is cross-cutting and underlies the other three. Exam tip: not sequential steps like NIST CSF's Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond. |
| 3 | GDPR Article 22 — what right does it grant? | Right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing (including profiling) that produces legal or similarly significant effects, unless an exception applies (contract necessity, law, explicit consent). Exam tip: exceptions still require safeguards including human intervention and right to contest. |
| 4 | EU AI Act Article 14 — core requirement | High-risk AI systems must enable human oversight: ability to understand, monitor, intervene, override, or stop the system before harm occurs. Exam tip: oversight must be preventive/real-time, not just post-hoc auditing. |
| 5 | Membership inference attack — definition | Attack determining whether a specific record was part of a model's training dataset, often by analyzing confidence scores. Exam tip: mitigated by differential privacy and regularization. |
| 6 | Model inversion attack — definition | Attack that reconstructs approximations of training data (e.g., images, text) from model outputs or gradients. Exam tip: distinct from membership inference — inversion recreates data, not just confirms presence. |
| 7 | Differential privacy — purpose in ML training | Adds calibrated statistical noise to training data or gradients so no single record materially changes model output, limiting membership inference and inversion risk. Exam tip: privacy budget (epsilon) trades off privacy vs. model accuracy — lower epsilon = stronger privacy, more accuracy loss. |
| 8 | Data minimization principle — source and meaning | GDPR Article 5(1)(c): personal data must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the stated purpose. Exam tip: applies to AI training data collection, not just storage. |
| 9 | ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — what does it standardize? | First international standard for an AI Management System (AIMS) — establishes, implements, maintains, and continually improves organizational AI governance. Exam tip: analogous to ISO 27001 but for AI management, not security controls specifically. |
| 10 | SHAP vs LIME — key distinction | SHAP: game-theoretic (Shapley values), gives globally consistent, additive feature attributions. LIME: builds a local linear surrogate model around a single prediction for local explainability. Exam tip: both are post-hoc explainability tools for black-box models, not intrinsic interpretability. |
| 11 | Safety guardrail vs ethical control — distinguishing test question pattern | Guardrail = real-time technical/operational mechanism preventing harmful output NOW (content filter, rate limit, kill switch). Ethical control = governance-level, values-based principle or review process guiding decisions (ethics board, published principles, stakeholder consultation). |
| 12 | EU AI Act risk tiers — list all four | Unacceptable risk (banned), High-risk (strict obligations: data governance, documentation, human oversight, logging), Limited risk (transparency obligations, e.g., chatbot disclosure), Minimal risk (no specific obligations). Exam tip: know which obligations attach to which tier. |
